Imagine an abandoned stage.
Desolate and Helpless.
Where the lights do not run through the skies anymore.
Where the voices do not echo anymore.
Where the gleaming eyes of actors do not shine anymore.

As the curtains slowly fall, the eyes of many are filled with joy, sorrow, and anger that words cannot quite hold. As the stage turns silent, the stories do not end there but begin. With a slip of a tear from the eyes. With a giggle, spilling from the lips. With an anger that melts into the air, the theatre flows through every soul – young and old, loud, and soft. Yet, it is never satisfied, because it is ready to whisper the next story.

But what happens when there is no one to listen to its whispers?

The theatre waits.

It waits for the footsteps that will reach the stage. It waits for the hands to brush the dust off its face. It waits for the ears to listen again to its whispers. It waits, knowing that the world has not forgotten how to create; it has simply forgotten to take those few steps into the light.

The theatre does not ask for the loudest voice or the brightest actor, or the most brilliant story. It longs for someone who is willing to begin.

All it needs is someone who is willing to take the first step into the light.

And that is the Drama Collective. Not one person who is brave, but many who are willing to take that step together.

We are not a mere group of people who want to just simply tell a story on the stage but breathe life into theatre away from the stage as well. Through workshops, we want to grow the seed of theatre in the minds of every student. Through community outreach, we do not want to simply give back to society; we want to draw it closer, embrace it, and make it part of our story.

Here, theatre is not something we simply watch. It is something we want to become.

It is a home where stories are waiting to be told, voices are ready to be found, and strangers call themselves a family.

Here, the whispers turn into loud echoes that vibrate in every corner. Our echoes would not stop once the lights are switched off or when the curtains are drawn. They would creep into everyday conversations, unlikely friendships and voices resonating with confidence. We do not want to leave behind students who know theatre. We want to leave behind hearts that have fallen in love with it and are ready to listen to its whispers. We want the theatre to find a home within each of them so that it can never escape from the thoughts of the modern generation.

And perhaps, this is where our biggest contribution to the school lies. Not in the performances we staged, but in the people we have changed. In students who have the courage to speak, patience to listen, and the imagination to create, we make the school more than a building filled with classrooms and corridors. Because a school is only about the people who fill it. And if our theatre, your theatre, can make these people a little more connected, our job is done. We have spread our echoes far beyond the boundaries of the stage.

As the stage blends into the community and the community paints a brighter future, every thought, every hope, and every action must have started from one single person at a single point in time. The Drama Collective is also the same. It started as a collective seed amongst three people, Aadi Ramnani, Laasya Pammi, and Yashasvini Alampally. You see the seed of the theatre, but they saw the tree before the seed was even sown. They saw the Collective as a family to grow rather than as a community to just remind theatre to students.

With us in play, theatre shall not become astray as we, the Drama Collective, will always be ready to display. Come what may!
