drama /ˈdraːmə/ noun
1 A play for theatrical performance. Everything to do with stage and storytelling and grandeur.
drama /ˈdraːmə/ noun
2 An exciting event. Nothing grand or performative. Just a little out of the ordinary.

One word. Two definitions. Under these mere explanations lies its undiscovered soul. Ancient. Sacred. Sublime. Hidden in its plain words lies the human life that seeks an escape from normalcy and monotony. Yet neither of the definitions stand false as an invisible bridge stays intact between these literal and creative perspectives. And that is emotion. Something which cannot be spoken or described. Only felt and showed.

Perhaps that is why drama as an art form has survived the test of time while many others have stopped evolving. Because at its core is the thing that makes us human – the ability to feel. Not just for ourselves but for the tragedies of others. Because tragedies do not differentiate between gods and humans. They only draw the line between the strong and weak during times of retreat.

But is it just the tragedy that makes drama so appealing to the human heart? What about the myriads of emotions like joy, grief, hope, love, hatred, fear, and contentment?

The stage allows these emotions to spill out freely and loudly. It is because life outside the stage often tells them to mellow down, restricting them to express only in ways that are acceptable.

However, on stage, these emotions get to unwind in their rawest forms. As monologues, allegories, and dramatic pauses echo in the minds of the audience, these emotions have no obligation to be sugar-coated.

For, in a performance, the audience is allowed to feel.

And that is what differentiates drama from every other art form because it does not make it far less complicated to rejoice for or mourn fictional characters.

It pulls the thoughts of the most important people in the room. The audience.

A play is incomplete without the participation of the audience. While other art forms possess the liberty of giving time and reflection to let the audience’s emotions flow naturally, drama evokes these emotions in real time, making the performance itself a collective human experience.

Now, let us drift towards the second definition of drama that has nothing to do with stage and acting. It is just the word we like to use when life decides to bless us with something exciting or challenging.

Something that jolts us awake from the slumbers of monotony and comfort in which we have put ourselves in.

Comfort is nice, but drama transforms life into more than just existence in relief.

It turns into more than excitement. It turns into love that I think can be attributed to the inherent need to find meaning in everything.

As humans, we like to believe that everything has a purpose and that meaning can be attributed to every joy and pain. Drama on stage just happens to be a tangible reflection of that need.

So, perhaps, drama never had two definitions. It is just one complex idea split into two meanings to show how art is a reflection of life and vice versa. Whether we are waiting for the curtains to be drawn or waiting for a monotonous day to get exciting, we are doing it for the same reason.

Perhaps the real hamartia is something we all share…the desire to find meaning.
