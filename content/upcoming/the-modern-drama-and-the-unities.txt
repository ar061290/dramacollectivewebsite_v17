The Modern Drama and the Unities
Drama is action getting ignited, time running in front of a character, and place unfolding in the minds of the audience.
But is this trio still alive in today’s world of dramaturgy?
Yes, it is still alive. However, they are no longer the rigid rules as once showcased in 1514 by Trissino in his play Sofonisba, which told its story only across 24 hours, anchored itself to a fixed place and focused its action only towards a singular plotline.
However, we must also drift across a century to 1609 to see the condition of the three unities; here, we see Shakespeare’s The Winter’s Tale. It formally transforms and shatters every unity. It premieres a 16-year long jump in time, breaking the 24-hour clock; it shifts between a court in Sicilia and a countryside in Bohemia, crossing every possible shoreline; and it merges a tragic drama with a lighthearted comedy forgetting the focus towards a singular, sharp action in the plot.
The three unities? So, are they Forgotten or Flexible?
They are neither. But they exist somewhere between these two states. They are not a set of rigid rules. Still, they are not left behind because they are now a theatre tool rather than a rule.
For writers, it is a creative tool wherein they use it to tighten a scene: not with bolts, however! They use it to remove the unnecessary elements present in the plot and increase the pace and impact at which the narration is happening. We can still see the unities in real-time TV shows and single room stage plays. One, using the unities heightens the drama. Second, it reduces the budget!
If plays can be written without the unities, then why do we need them?
Plays do not always exist without the unities. It is that they are not seen. To observe the hidden unities, we now turn to Shakespeare’s Macbeth. Even though it abandons all the three unities, in Act 3 Scene 4 (Banquet scene) Macbeth is confined to a single location; he keeps a close eye at the clock making the scene last only until the dinner is completed at the banquet; and the plot only focuses on Macbeth and his guests at the banquet.
This is exactly where the unities stand today. Not to barge into and mold every script but to make special entrances that make the thrill of the theatre live on.
The Unities have not disappeared. They have simply changed their role – from rules that govern play to tools that carve them.
Action still ignites.
Time still runs.
Place still unfolds.
But today, the playwright decides when, where, and how.

By Kodela Gautam
