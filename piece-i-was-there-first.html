I Was There First

By Kathya Yaratapalli

I was there first. When I was younger I had a lot of friends and suddenly this new kid with glasses and a very odd personality comes in and starts stirring up fights. She was stupid and it seems her stupidity spread to all the others or maybe they were already stupid and she just triggered the reaction. She was the root cause, she was despicable and I didn’t like her but apparently me not liking her led to everybody not liking me.

I am a very nice person so when I have a problem with someone just imagining how horrible they would be with fake smiles and gifts (which were just as fake). There were six of us, good friends, not too many fights and then the seventh one came along the road and changed everything for the worse. So seven slowly became six as I drifted off like a plane gone off course and of course soon after that I felt pretty guilty about leaving and I went ‘hey, my decision was a little premature, mind letting me back into the gang’ and they were all ‘Oh my god we knew you’d come around’.

So I was briefly happy, I had friends but I knew I was lying to myself because I didn’t like my friends, they made me cringe. I knew I had to do something but there wasn’t actually anything I could do. I was confused, I felt a mix of feelings and didn’t know which ones to act on. My insides felt like they were dirty and I didn’t know how to clean them up.

Looking back, I think they were all horrible influences. Nearly all of them got absolutely terrible grades in school, they didn’t get any sort of physical activity and worst of all they based people’s social status on how many electronic devices they had. The worst part was that everybody in the gang had a ‘best friend’ and I felt as if I needed a best friend in this gang to make myself fit in. I had a weird idea that this hypothetical ‘best friend’ would give me happiness and a sense of belonging in the gang.

And so the madness began. I went one person after the other trying to bond with them somewhat to understand their mindset. So I tried to like what they liked to do what they thought was ‘cool’ and it did not work. I ended up learning that there was a good side and a bad side to people. Nobody was all good or all bad people were in fact both good and bad. I didn’t understand this at first because I had convinced myself that these kids were absolutely and completely evil to the bone.

But it wasn’t true. What they were doing, in their own mind, was what they thought was good and for all I know they could be at home thinking I was wrong but the truth is we’ll never know who’s wrong because I will always believe that I am right and they will always believe that they are right and that’s the way the world works because if you dislike someone so much you will eventually convince yourself that they are wrong and you are right and that’s actually really interesting.

So in the end I realised it didn’t really matter that it didn’t really matter who was there first because people need to know how to move on in life and soon no one will care who was there first.
