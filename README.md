# The Drama Collective · website (v12)

The home page for the CHIREC Drama Collective: one page, a dark theatre before curtain-up. A marble statue stands in a fixed three.js scene inside the Penrose ring while the page scrolls past it. The statue performs:

1. she makes a full revolution, the mask still raised;
2. as she turns on, one and a half revolutions more until she faces you, she brings the tragedy mask down beside her face;
3. she turns her head away from it;
4. the view closes in on her upper body: her head, the hand and the mask;
5. in the close-up her marble flows into the Collective's mark, carved in the same stone, part for part and where each part is: her mask becomes the logo's mask, her hand the hand that holds it, her face the profile face, and the three hold for a moment on the marble figure; then, as her robes stream into the brush strokes, flames and crescent, the mark grows out to fill the ring and the view opens out again;
6. meanwhile one more star shows in the sky behind the stage, comes forward through the ring and settles beside the mask as the logo's own star.

The home page has a hero and five acts: the Collective's statement, a countdown to the production, this month's publication, what the Collective does (five cards the scroll turns), and the footer's own columns, which dock into the footer with the logo. The hero's meta description is still placeholder.

## What changed in v19: performance, second round

Nothing on the page looks or behaves differently. Measured back to back against the v18 build on the lab's phone (390×844, the CPU four times slower), with the pixel guardrail at threshold 0 on the pages concerned.

- **The writers page boots lighter.** Its opening put a new picture of the writers section on the book's first page at every ScrollTrigger refresh: seven times before the curtain had parted, fourteen in a visit with a filter and a resize. Each new picture is restyled and laid out, 30–50 ms on a phone. Now the copy is made and compared with the picture as it was made; when they are the same, the picture stays and only its size and place are set again (`paint()` in `src/writers/opening.js`): once at the boot, three times in that visit. The opening's ResizeObserver no longer refreshes the page for a report of the size the section had when the places were last taken, which its first report always is (about 100 ms on a phone). On the phone's page boot (median of three, run in both orders): style recalculations 444 → 299–324, tasks over 50 ms 2.6–2.7 s → 1.9–2.0 s, DOM nodes 3590 → 3081; style and layout from the navigation to 4 s after the curtain 2.0–2.2 s → 1.7 s. The picture is checked against a fresh copy, and against v18's, at every step of a visit (the boot, through the hold and back, a filter, a resize), on a phone and a laptop: the same every time.
- **A Fillout form no longer refreshes the page on its first report.** Its ResizeObserver was meant to skip its first call (the box as the page was set up and measured with it) but started from −1, so that call always refreshed the page's scroll-linked effects: about 100 ms on the publication page on a phone, where the refreshing to 3 s after the curtain went from 390 ms to 150–250 ms. The form's growing still refreshes them. On the publication, write, apply and sponsor pages every ScrollTrigger's start and end, once settled, is v18's.
- **Tried and set aside: the sky drawn after the frame's layout.** Drawing it from a ResizeObserver it provokes, so that reading where the text blocks are never forces a style recalculation, was exact (its masks matched the frame's boxes to the pixel) but gained nothing measurable on About's phone scroll (busy 77% either way), for an extra element and DOM writes in every frame. Not kept.
- **Found, for the owner: the music bars.** While the music plays, the four bars' CSS animation (on SVG rects) restyles and lays out the top bar in every frame, and every script that reads the layout in that frame does it first: ScrollTrigger reads the scroll position in its scroll handler, the sky reads its text blocks. On About's phone scroll that is about a fifth of each frame's work (callbacks, style and layout 11.2 → 9.1 ms a frame with the bars still; busy 5 points less). The `compositor-bars` switch (v18) takes back the layout part (11.2 → 9.6 ms); the style part stays, because while the page runs any frame loop (the sky does) Chrome refreshes a running animation's style in every frame, composited or not. Whether the switch's bars look the same on a real phone is the owner's call.
- **Looked at, nothing invisible to take: the home page standing still on a phone** (68-79% busy). The stage draws nothing then; the rest is the browser redrawing what moves, about 2 ms a frame of layering alone: the scroll cue (a looping Lottie, already paused once out of sight), the music bars while the music plays, and the sky's twinkle every other frame. The stage reading the scroll position in every frame forces that frame's style early, but held to one value it saved nothing.
- **The lab:** new forensics (`tools/perf/README.md`): the order of a frame's callbacks and what each forced, what dirtied style during a scroll, every style recalculation and layout of a boot by its cause, two builds compared from the curtain parting on, a function profile of a load. The guardrail now holds the writer statue still as well and waits for an embedded form's frame to keep its height; shot twice, one build now differs only in one team photo's filtering (at most 5 of 255) and the write page's statue (at most 1 of 255).

## What changed in v18: performance

Nothing on the page looks or behaves differently; every change below is measured in the lab (`tools/perf/`, its own README) against the v17 build, with pixel-by-pixel screenshots of every page at four viewports as the guardrail (`tools/perf/visual.mjs`: the sky, the stages and every animation held at one instant; the publication banner's rolling line is hidden for the shot, since it keeps its own time).

- **The lab** (`tools/perf/`): benchmarks of the site's own journeys (the home page's loading to curtain-up, every page's boot to its curtain parting, four seconds of stillness, a scroll to the foot, a pointer sweep, the theme switch, a link through the curtain, the cost of one style change, what a page holds in memory), run in the installed Chrome, headless, on the real GPU, against the built site served as GitHub Pages serves it (`--h2` serves over HTTP/2 as Pages does, which every run with a network profile should use: over HTTP/1.1 the browser's six connections queue the many small requests). `npm run perf`, `npm run perf:gate`, `npm run perf:visual`. The site sets `performance.mark('dc:…')` at its own steps (`src/util/perf.js`: `boot`, `stage code`, `statue decoded`, `shaders compiled`, `curtain up`, `morph ready`…; the inner pages' `boot`, `fonts`, `setup`, `parted`), which `?debug` still prints.
- **The lab, second pass (20 October):** both builds are measured back to back in short runs (a number from another time of day is no comparison: the graphics driver's shader cache, warm or cold, moved the home page's start by seconds). A still page is measured with nothing of the lab's running in it: its in-page frame counter, a `requestAnimationFrame` loop, kept still pages producing frames, and in every frame Chrome refreshes the style of every running animation, so the idle numbers before included work the lab caused; jank on a still page is read from the browser's own long-animation-frame entries, after the page has settled. The phone's scroll is a real touch drag (a synthesised touch scroll gesture does not scroll a page in headless Chrome: the phone's scroll numbers before measured drags at the top of the page). The network ledger keeps each request's start and end, for waterfalls. New forensics in `tools/perf/forensics/` name the loop, the function, the program or the pixel behind a number (its README lists them).
- **Field measurements and the gate:** `src/util/field.js` reports what the visitor's browser saw (the site's marks, paint timings, long tasks, layout shift, device class, WebGL context losses, discards) once per visit as the page is put away, to an endpoint named in the head; the pages carry none unless written with `FIELD_ENDPOINT` set (`FIELD_ENDPOINT=https://… node tools/pages/build.mjs`), and then nothing is sent. Choosing the endpoint is the owner's. `.github/workflows/perf.yml` runs the lab's boot and idle benches on every push and keeps the results; it gates once a baseline has been ratcheted in.
- **Deploying the build:** `.github/workflows/pages.yml` builds with Vite and publishes `dist/` to GitHub Pages; `vite.config.js` has `base: './'`, so the build works at a domain's root and under a project path alike. The live site today serves the source tree as-is (115 requests and an import map to jsDelivr for the home page; the build is 48 requests, minified and tree-shaken). Turning it on is a setting on the repository (Pages → Source: GitHub Actions).
- **The home page's start:** the loader's satin shader is set up just after the first paint (a timeout from the first frame) and linked in the background (`KHR_parallel_shader_compile`), instead of holding the parser for most of a second before any script ran; the stage compiles every program the first frame needs (the shadow pass's and the silhouette's included, with stand-ins keyed exactly like the real passes) and uploads its textures while the driver compiles, so the first frame compiles nothing; and those programs are compiled while the statue is still downloading (`Stage.preWarm`: stand-ins with one-pixel textures on three-vertex meshes (the ring in both its states, translucent while it reveals itself and opaque after), which three.js keys exactly as it keys the real materials, so by the time the statue has arrived only its textures remain to upload); three.js's shader-error checks are off unless `?debug`. The morph mesh (183k triangles) is computed in a worker (`src/scene/morphCore.js`, `morphWorker.js`; the SVG is parsed on the page, since workers have no DOM, and its shapes travel as data), then its vertices go to the GPU and its programs compile in a quiet moment (`Stage.warmMorph`: the morph, the logo star and its glow are shown for one draw into a one-pixel target keyed like the screen, their shadow-pass and silhouette programs compiled in the background from stand-ins first, so nothing links when the morph reaches the screen), where before the build held the page for two and a half seconds during the intro and the first morph frame stalled again. The statue's silhouette (for the sky) is read back asynchronously. The main script lets the loader paint before its own set-up (the sky, the WebGL probe and the stage's import took a tenth of a second before the first frame could be painted).
- **Every page, every frame:** GSAP's ScrollTrigger keeps an empty `requestAnimationFrame` loop going for as long as it is enabled (for Firefox, its source says); in Chrome it kept every still page producing a frame 60 times a second, and with every frame the browser refreshed the style of every running animation. The build leaves that loop out in Chromium browsers only (`quietScrollTrigger()` in `vite.config.js`; the build stops if GSAP's code there changes). The music button's four bars are four small SVGs, the same rectangles at the same places (pixel-identical in every state), so that a trial switch can move their animation to the compositor (below). The publication banner's rolling line is a Web Animation on the compositor (it was moved from script, a style recalculation and a layout per frame on every page that has it); the star sky looks its text blocks up once a second (and when the markup changes) rather than every frame, leaves out the stars that lie wholly under a text block, and draws every other frame while nothing is happening (the slow twinkle does not show it; the pointer, the scroll or a constellation bring it back to every frame). Each star is still drawn exactly as it was: filling them in batches by brightness was tried and taken out again, because Chrome's rasteriser rounds a batched circle differently and the picture changed by a few pixels.
- **Bytes:** the favicon and the top bar's fallback mark are a 9 KB, 144 px copy of the 117 KB mark (`assets/brand/mark-gold-144.png`); the inner pages preload the four faces they paint with (Raung, Lato 400 and 700, Open Sans 400), so the text reveals split their lines on the real type once rather than on the fallback and again; the home page preloads only Raung, as before (its text is first painted after the curtain, seconds in, and the other preloads only competed with its stylesheet).
- **Loading, every page:** lottie-web is fetched the first time a Lottie is asked for (always after the curtain; it was the deepest module of every page's boot graph) and each file's JSON once; the WebGL probe runs once per page and its result is shared (the publication, the reading pages, About and the earrings each made their own throwaway context, some before the curtain); the reading pages' masks import three.js only after the curtain has parted and probe WebGL only on screens wide enough to show them; the next page is prefetched when the pointer settles on a link or presses it (speculation rules, prefetch only) and again as the curtain closes; the source-served site connects to jsDelivr at once and loads the minified three.js; the music file lost its 366 KB of unused cover art (every page downloaded it before the random start could be chosen). The build's home page makes the statue's preload only for the devices that will draw the live stage (Save-Data, two cores or two gigabytes or less, no WebGL 2 get the still picture, and used to download 1.81 MB first), and starts the statue from a script in the first bytes of the head (lower down it ran only after the stylesheet, and on a slow link the model started late), and the Draco decoder and the stage's chunks once the loader has painted (in the first bytes they shared a slow link with the stylesheet and held the loader's first paint back by a quarter of a second on 4G); the publication preloads the still for the theme in use.
- **Every frame, inner pages:** the sponsor page's metal light reads every piece's box (and the viewport) before it writes any style: one style flush a frame while a pointer moves, instead of five; the cursor's drawn star is positioned only while it is shown and the scroll's hit test joins the frame's update; the follow-spot writes its custom properties only where a lit copy of the text reads them and leaves GSAP's ticker when nothing moves; the countdown rests off the screen; the vector logo's colours are written only when they change; the text reveals and the forms' resize no longer trigger a second ScrollTrigger refresh; the earrings are 'near' only when the top bar is on the screen and draw on the low-power GPU; the coming-soon curtain draws without a multisampled back buffer it never used, skips the glow passes while the glow is at zero, draws every other frame while nothing but the slow sway is going on, and the star sky under it stops while the curtain fills the screen; About builds the lexicon's fields from the moment the curtain parts (a quick scroll used to meet a synchronous build) and its ring skips the two stone matcaps it never used.
- **The publication's and the writers' statues:** each compiles its shadow pass's depth program with the rest (a stand-in keyed like the real pass) instead of on its first frame, and keeps that stand-in's material until the first draw has used the program (letting it go first released the program, and the first draw compiled it again: 0.6-1 s on a first visit); the Thinker's model is prepared in place, without a copy, its bounds computed once at the end (`?debug` checks every number against the old way); the statue pages mark their steps (`dc:statue-env`, `-model`, `-compiled`, `-drawn`); the first frame is drawn once per mount (the theme is re-applied only if it changed while loading); the lean's easing ends at a sub-pixel step by landing on its target in one last frame rather than creeping toward it for a second of full redraws; a scroll under a finger that has lifted redraws nothing (the cursor's lamp is out); the writer statue reads its box's place once per scroll, where the layout is settled, rather than inside every drawn frame, and the resize observer's first call no longer draws a second first frame.
- **The home page, once up:** the statue's measures are made in the same passes as before, each exactly as three.js computes them, but each pass reads each bone's matrix once instead of once per vertex and influence (`vertexReader` in `src/scene/statue.js`; `?debug` checks every vertex and every box against three.js's own, bit for bit; skinning once and re-using the vertices across the passes, as tried first, moved the statue's framing by a fraction of a pixel); the footer dock computes where its columns are from its measures and the scroll instead of four layout reads interleaved with style writes on every scroll event; the stage reads the footer's top once per scroll position (and again when the layout changes) rather than on every tick; the hero's scroll cue pauses while the hero is off the screen. On phones, while the Menu sheet covers the page, the sky rests and the stage settles and stops, as it does behind the footer. The publication and writers pages save the visitor's place when the tab goes into the background too (a phone may discard a tab without ever firing pagehide), and the inner pages take their pieces down last-in-first-out, each on its own, from the first moment of boot.
- **Trial switches (changes that would show, off unless turned on):** `?flags=name` on any page turns a trial change on in that browser (kept in `localStorage` as `dc-flags`), `?flags=` turns them all off; each sets `html.flag-<name>` before the first paint. `compositor-bars`: the music bars animate the four SVGs on the compositor instead of the rectangles on the page's thread (no layout per frame while the music plays; at rest the button is pixel-identical, while the bars move their edges are resampled by the GPU, a fraction of a pixel).
  `phone-tier` (touch screens only): a pixel budget of 2 million device pixels for a full screen for the home stage, About's ring and the sky (a 1024x1366 iPad at DPR 2 draws at ratio 1.2); at most 60 frames a second however fast the screen (the home stage, the sky, the coming-soon curtain); the home morph built once the visitor has scrolled half a screen; after 30 s in a hidden tab the home stage's WebGL context let go and taken again on return, the still picture showing meanwhile (`tools/perf/forensics/phone-tier.mjs` tries all of it).
- **The owner's decisions (20 October), as recommended:**
  - **The music starts once the curtain is up** (the loader lifted, an inner page's curtain parted): until then the element is asked to play with no file, so a browser that refuses says so at once (the button waits for the visitor, whose first tap, click or key starts it, as ever), and one that allows it fetches the file, and the media controls their artwork, only after the curtain (`tools/perf/forensics/music.mjs`).
  - **The earrings on intent on touch screens, slow links and Save-Data:** there the model and three.js are fetched together when the theme switch is pressed or focused, not at idle on every page, and the earring comes down if ready within 8 s (3 s where they were fetched ahead). Laptops and desktops still fetch them at idle.
  - **A failsafe:** a page that cannot start is shown as it is without script (no loader, no closed curtain, the intro's parts shown, the scroll free): its script never arrived or threw as it loaded (the head notices 8 s after the page has loaded, from a flag the script sets, `window.dcBooted`), or its start threw before it was up (at once); a home statue that stops arriving (no bytes for 20 s, time hidden or frozen not counted) gives way to the still picture. The planner has its own. `tools/perf/forensics/failsafe.mjs` causes each failure on purpose.
  - **Field measurements ready:** `.github/workflows/pages.yml` writes the pages with the repository variable `FIELD_ENDPOINT` (and `FIELD_SAMPLE`, one visit in ten if unset); `tools/field/` holds a free receiver (a Google Apps Script that appends each report to a Google Sheet) and its set-up. Reports go without cookies. Nothing is sent until the variable is set.
  - **Deploying:** the workflow builds on every push to `main` and deploys only when the repository's Pages site is set to be published by GitHub Actions.

QA flags:

- `?debug` adds a slider that scrubs the statue's whole sequence from 0 to 1 without scrolling, with buttons for each phase and a readout of every track (the close-up's `zoom` among them) and of how far the logo has grown;
- `?static` forces the still picture;

## The statue sequence

One number, the scroll progress through `<main>` (0 at the top, 1 where the footer begins), drives everything. The sequence is laid out in **screens of scroll** (one viewport height each, `LENGTHS` in [`src/scene/sequence.js`](src/scene/sequence.js)); `retime()` turns them into the phases (`PHASES`) for the page's own scroll length, measured on every refresh. The morph and the logo keep their lengths whatever the page; the hero, the turn and the close-up share what is left. The home page's `<main>` is at least 6.4 screens tall (5.4 screens of scroll), the designed length, so every phase runs at its designed pace:

| Phase | Screens | Progress | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| hero | 0.2 | 0–0.037 | v2's composition at rest: seen from behind, mask raised high |
| revolve + pose | 2.12 | 0.037–0.43 | the turn (the first revolution with the mask raised, then the pose); twice v3's speed |
| ↳ pose | | 0.21–0.43 | she turns on, a revolution and a half more, to face the viewer; through it the **arm** brings the mask beside the face (cubic, 5–80% of the phase) and the **head** turns away (quad, 35–100%) |
| zoom | 0.28 | 0.43–0.48 | the view closes in on her upper body (sine in-out), until her head, hand and mask, with the logo's that will form on them, fill 45% of the view's height (or 70% of its width, if that is less, as on a phone); twice v3's speed |
| morph | 2.24 | 0.48–0.90 | in the close-up, the statue's surface flows into the 3D logo: mask, hand and face in turn, at her head; then the robes stream into the rest of the mark as the logo grows to its place and the view opens out again; v3's pace |
| logo | 0.56 | 0.90–1 | the stone logo holds in the ring, swaying slowly away and back, with a gentle idle drift; v3's pace |

The turn is one smooth motion across the revolve and pose phases: 2.5 revolutions (900°) through one sine ease, so it never pauses between revolutions, and the pose phase starts exactly where the ease completes the first revolution (`TURN` in `sequence.js`: change `revolutions`, keeping it on a half, or `first`).

End of the pose, as the viewer sees it: she faces you, the mask is on the right of the screen at face height, angled toward you, and her face looks to the left.

The close-up is a zoom of the picture itself: the camera stays where it is and renders a smaller window of the same view (`setViewOffset`). The window frames her head, hand and mask and the logo's forming on them (before the morph mesh is built, a point between her head and her hand, easing over to the real framing if the mesh arrives mid-close-up), and puts them where the statue's centre would be in the composition, brought in from the composition's sideways offset as far as the close-up needs to stay on screen (portrait tablets, which get the desktop layout). The light, the shadows and the ring are untouched. As the logo grows, the zoom eases back out geometrically, on the morph's own schedule even if the morph mesh never builds, and the window returns to the plain view: the logo grows on screen only from the close-up's share of the view to its final size (about 1.4× on desktop), not fourfold, while the ring comes back into view around it (`CLOSE_UP` in `Stage.js`). The cursor parallax is divided by the zoom, so it moves the picture no more in the close-up than out of it, and a drag or the lean toward the cursor turns her inside a steady frame.

The sequence is scrubbed (scrub 1.2) and fully reversible. With reduced motion nothing is scrubbed: the hero pose holds, then the logo appears in one step when the morph section is reached.

Other stage motion carries over from v2:

- the ring makes four turns about its own centre over the page (`RING_TURNS` in `src/sections/scenes.js`);
- the camera arcs 22° and moves closer; during the morph the turntable takes up that arc, so the logo ends square to the camera;
- the light moves warm, cool, warm by section;
- the stage steps left and right so the text sits in empty space.

On top of the sequence, the statue still answers the mouse:

- she turns up to 10° toward the cursor;
- over her, the key light leans in and the cursor changes to its grab form;
- a sideways drag turns her, with a fling, and the turn eases back to the choreography afterwards.

### Ring clearance

The ring never crosses the statue or the logo. The statue's bounding sphere is centred on her turning axis, so it holds at any turn. It is measured over both the rest pose and the end pose (both inside a radius of 1.094 statue units; the height is 2). The logo is scaled so that it, plus the morph's mid-flight rise, fits inside the same sphere. Where it first forms, at her head, it is smaller and placed so that it stays inside its own sphere (with room for the parts' small shifts onto hers), and every point of its growth lies between the two. The morph's swirl turns about the same axis, which keeps every point's distance from the centre. Camera distance and field of view keep that sphere inside the ring's hole at every point of the scroll, as in v2. The close-up does not change this: it magnifies the whole picture, ring included, so the statue stays inside the hole, which simply reaches past the edges of the screen.

On narrow (portrait) screens the finished logo (about 1.7 units wide) takes up most of the view's width, about 90% on a 390 × 844 phone. As the logo grows, the turntable scales down about the centre of the clearance sphere (on the turning axis, where the finished logo is centred) until the logo fits 86% of the visible width; because it scales about the sphere's centre, making it smaller only helps the clearance.

## The rig (`tools/blender/`)

The source is a Meshy scan: one fused mesh with no skeleton, where the mask, hand, arm, head, robes and plinth are a single connected surface. It has 502,574 vertices, 941,264 triangles and three WebP textures, and it is 20.4 MB. The mask is in the figure's **left** hand, so the 180° turn alone puts it on the right of the screen.

`build_rig.py` makes `assets/models/statue-rig.glb` (**1.81 MB**: 79,976 vertices, 160,000 triangles, Draco-compressed, 2048 px WebP textures):

1. **Prepare the mesh.** Import, merge the UV-seam duplicates (so the surface is connected), normalise (height 2, base on y = 0, plinth footprint on the y axis), and decimate to 160k triangles.
2. **Skeleton.** Built from [`rig.json`](tools/blender/rig.json): root, spine, neck, head, left clavicle, upper arm, forearm (two bones, so turning the mask twists along the arm instead of pinching the wrist) and hand, plus a static bone for the right arm. Joints were placed from orthographic renders made with `inspect_views.py`.
3. **Weights.** Blender's automatic (bone heat) weights, then:
   - the hand and mask become one rigid piece, blended over the grip;
   - nothing below the chest follows the arm or head, so the robes stay put;
   - 4 influences per vertex, normalised.
4. **End pose**, from [`pose.json`](tools/blender/pose.json):
   - **arm:** analytic two-bone IK puts the wrist beside the jaw with the elbow down;
   - **hand:** turns the mask to face the viewer, angled a little out, and keeps it upright;
   - **head:** nods forward 30° from its upturned gaze and turns 55° away; the neck takes 35% of the turn.
5. **Export.** A GLB with one action, `Pose` (frame 0 = rest, frame 10 = end). The site reads the two keyframes per bone and blends the arm and the head on their own curves.

Rebuild after changing `rig.json` or `pose.json`:

```bash
blender -b --factory-startup -P tools/blender/build_rig.py -- <source.glb> assets/models/statue-rig.glb tris=160000 tex=2048 renders=<dir>
```

## The 3D logo (`tools/logo/`, `src/scene/logo.js`)

`trace_logo.ps1` thresholds the alpha of `assets/brand/mark-gold.png` (Blender reads the PNG, `png_to_pbm.py`) and traces it with potrace into `assets/brand/mark.svg` (48 KB, 21 paths):

```powershell
powershell -File tools/logo/trace_logo.ps1 -Blender <blender.exe> -Potrace <potrace.exe>
```

At load time `logo.js` builds the 3D mark:

- **extrusion:** `SVGLoader` + `ExtrudeGeometry`, 7% of its width deep with a small bevel;
- **orientation:** turned to read correctly from the viewer's side;
- **size:** scaled to fit the ring-clearance sphere;
- **shading:** creased normals, so the caps stay flat and the bevels smooth;
- **parts:** each triangle is labelled with the part of the mark its centroid falls in. The caps are triangulated as fans of long slivers, so first every cap triangle that straddles two parts is split at the middle of its longest edge until it lies in one (or is under 8 SVG units long); otherwise slivers from the brush strokes would cut across the profile face.

The curves are sampled as finely as the morph's triangle budget allows.

## The morph (`src/scene/morph.js`)

The morph is one mesh with the same triangles in two shapes, changing part for part. It is not a cross-fade, and two meshes are never visible at once.

The logo is about four times the size of her head, hand and mask together, so it does not form at its full size. It forms **at her head** first, in the close-up, a quarter of its size, laid over her so that its mask lies on her mask, its hand on her hand and its face on her face. Each part changes where it is, into its counterpart at about its own size, and the three hold for a moment on the marble figure. Then, as the robes stream into the brush strokes, flames and crescent, the parts close up into the logo's own layout and the logo grows out to its place in the ring.

| Statue | becomes | Logo |
|---|---|---|
| the mask in her hand | → | the logo's mask: the D-shaped frame around it, the eye, the nose and lips |
| her hand and forearm | → | the hand holding the mask, down its long wrist |
| her head and neck | → | the profile face |
| torso, robes, upper arm, the other arm, plinth | → | the brush strokes, the flames and the crescent |
| (nothing: it comes from the sky) | → | the star beside the mask |

- **The statue's parts** come from the rig. The hand bone carries the hand and, above the grip, the mask; the forearm bones join the hand (in the end pose her forearm runs down from the wrist to the elbow, like the logo's long wrist), and the upper arm stays with the body; the head and neck bones are the face (`statueParts` in `morph.js`).
- **The logo's parts** are polygons drawn over the trace in [`assets/brand/mark-regions.json`](assets/brand/mark-regions.json). In the mark, the mask is drawn as negative space, so its part is the frame and features around it. The profile face is negative space too, so its part is the profile with the gold it is cut from (the right edge of the brush strokes): it reads as a face even small, and her nose becomes its nose.
- **Checking the split.** `tools/logo/regions_preview.py` renders the logo coloured by part, and `tools/logo/components.py` lists its separate ink pieces. With `?debug`, "Show parts" tints both shapes during the morph (mask gold, hand green, face blue, body grey).
- **Where it forms.** `fitOverHead` in `morph.js` finds the uniform scale and shift (no turn) that best carry the bounds of the logo's mask, hand and face onto hers, as the viewer sees them: a least-squares fit over each box's centre and corners. One fit cannot place all three (in the logo the hand lies between the mask and the face; on her it lies under the mask), so each part also gets the small shift that puts its centre on hers (at most 0.07 units), which fades out as the parts close up. The logo stands level with the front of her three parts, so her marble chest does not cover it. With the current rig it forms at 0.25 of its size, centred just beside her face. The fit is computed at load from the rig and the regions, so it follows any change to either.
- **Order and timing**, as shares of the morph: the mask changes first, almost as one piece (0–0.18), then the hand (0.08–0.26), then the face (0.16–0.38). Her upper arm belongs to the body (the logo's hand is a hand and a wrist), but it leaves with the hand, from the elbow up (0.14–0.42), so no stump is left behind. The three new parts hold on the marble figure (to 0.46). Then the robes stream on a wider swirl into the rest of the mark (0.46–1), the parts close up into the logo's own layout (0.46–0.62), and the logo grows from her head to its place (0.55–1, smoothstep; its scale grows geometrically, its centre moves in a straight line). The timings are in `MORPH.parts`, `MORPH.upperArm`, `MORPH.settle` and `MORPH.grow`; a part is complete at `start + spread + travel`.

How it is built:

- **Start shape.** Each triangle starts as one of the statue's triangles in its end pose. The positions are computed on the CPU from the same skeleton the GPU skins with. At the start of the morph phase, in the close-up (about 3×), the skinned statue is hidden and this mesh is shown. Measured on the canvas there, the swap changes nothing visible: a mean difference of 0.002 per pixel, with 4 pixels of 1.28 million (on her silhouette's edge) differing by more than 24/255, which is anti-aliasing; unzoomed, at most 5/255 on any pixel.
- **End shape.** Within each part, the logo's triangles are tessellated by longest-edge splits to the statue part's triangle count. Where the logo part is the larger, the statue part is padded with copies of its own triangles, which start exactly on top of each other. The mask and the hand are padded about 3× and 15×, so they spread out as they change. In total there are 183,587 triangles.
- **Pairing.** Within each part, triangles are paired by balanced recursive median splits of their centroids, each set normalised to its own bounds. Her face looks to the screen's left at the end of the pose, as the logo's profile does, so nose goes to nose and crown to brow. Each pair's corners are rotated to the best match, so triangles do not flip.
- **Motion.** Each triangle travels to its place in the logo, as the logo stands at that moment (at her head, growing, or in the ring), with a slight rise (in proportion for the mask, hand and face, which change at the logo's small size) and, for the body, a swirl about the turning axis. The logo's place is one uniform (`uFit`: centre and scale) plus a shift per part (`uShift`, faded by `uSettle`), so its growth costs nothing per triangle and does not touch the normals. The material stays the statue's own (same roughness, no metal, the stage's reflections); per triangle, the texture evens out to the stone's average colour and the normal map fades, since each logo triangle carries a stray patch of her texture.
- **Shaders.** The same vertex path runs in the colour pass, the shadow pass and the star-field silhouette pass. The mesh is built in about a second, after the hero is up; until then the statue simply holds its end pose (and the close-up still opens out on the morph's schedule).

The mask, hand and face change almost as solid pieces, each in its own place. The body comes apart into a flowing lace and reassembles as the brush strokes, flames and crescent, because one unbroken surface cannot become shapes with holes through them.

Retune it with the `MORPH` constants (part timings, the upper arm, the settle, the grow, swirl, rise). Redraw the parts in `mark-regions.json`.

### The star from the sky (`src/scene/star.js`)

The four-pointed star beside the mask is extruded on its own (`logo.js` takes the smallest shape of the trace around `star` in `mark-regions.json`; same depth and bevel as the mark) and is not part of the morph. As the robes start to stream (morph 0.36–0.44) it shows in the sky up and to the right of its place, far behind the ring, a few pixels of gold that twinkle like the sky's stars. From 0.44 to 0.95 it comes forward through the ring's opening, turning as it nears, burning gold and cooling to stone, to its place on the logo as the logo stands at that moment, and it lands with a glint (0.9–1). It is a stone star on the turntable plus a glow sprite (additive gold on the dark stage, gold-700 with house lights up), hidden behind the figure while it is behind her. Retune it with `STAR`.

## Performance and fallbacks

- **Loading:** the loader is in the page itself (`index.html`: the satin in raw WebGL at a third of the screen's pixels, the sunburst in SVG on a 75 ms timer), so it runs before any script loads. The 1.81 MB rig is preloaded with the page; three.js and the 48 KB logo trace load lazily behind the loader; the Draco decoder (`public/draco/`, from three's examples, WebAssembly only) is fetched and compiled while the rig downloads. The stage's shaders are compiled with `compileAsync` before the curtain goes up (`Stage.warm`), so the first frame does not stall. `?debug` prints how long each step took. The morph mesh is built in an idle moment after the intro.
- **Fonts:** WOFF2, converted from the design system's TTF/OTF with fontTools (same outlines; 300 KB in all, from 760 KB).
- **Geometry:** the skinned statue has 79,976 vertices and 160,000 triangles. The morph mesh has 550,761 vertices (183,587 independent triangles; about 40 MB of vertex data on the GPU). Only one of the two is drawn at a time.
- **Rendering:**
  - DPR is capped at 2;
  - a frame is rendered only when something changed (while the logo holds, its idle drift keeps rendering);
  - the shadow map is redrawn only when the shape or the turn changes;
  - both canvases pause when the tab is hidden or the footer covers the stage.
- **Static fallback:** no WebGL, a software renderer, Save-Data, 2 cores or fewer, or 2 GB of memory or less all get the still picture (fetched only then), with the star field kept.
- **Reduced motion:** no intro, parallax, ring turn or scrubbed sequence (the hero pose, then the logo in one step), no lean toward the cursor, and no fling.
- **Inner pages:** three.js loads only on the coming-soon and sponsor pages, and only where WebGL runs well (the same low-power check); otherwise a CSS velvet with the sign in type, and the mark masked in the tier's metal. The curtain renders while it is on screen; the medallion only while something moves. Pixel ratio 1.75 on the curtain, 2 on the medallion.
- **Teardown:** `dramaCollective.destroy()` (also on `pagehide`) frees the GPU memory, including the morph mesh, along with ScrollTriggers, SplitTexts, Lotties and listeners.
